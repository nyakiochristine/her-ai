# Part 1: How the skill builds a script

## Why these reels exist

her/ai's mission is AI literacy. When people understand what AI actually is, they stop treating it as magic or as a threat. They see it as a tool and use it with nuance and agency.

Every reel shows **one demo, one limitation and one message**, live on screen, in **40 seconds at most**. The viewer gets an "oh, *that's* why" moment and can copy the demo at home the same evening. Every reel is also a teaser for the her/ai workshops and webinars.

Content pillar: **practical education**. No hype and no doom.

## The mental model every reel reinforces

> **An AI system \= a large language model (the brain, baked once) \+ a harness (the tools around it: memory, web search, code, research) \+ an interface (the app).**

Most surprising AI behaviour is explained by which of these three parts is doing the work. The screen shows the behaviour, and the voice-over names the part responsible.

## What makes a good demo

A demo idea only qualifies if it passes all five tests:

1. **One message.** It fits in one sentence.  
2. **It happens live.** The tool really does it on screen, and nothing is mocked up.  
3. **It reveals a limitation or a mechanism.** For example: prediction rather than knowledge, the model vs. its tools, the training cut-off, bias, or following your framing.  
4. **Viewers can copy it tonight** with the named tool, at most after changing one setting.  
5. **It fits in 40 seconds**, including the time spent waiting for the AI.

Newer models sometimes pass old demos, so **every demo gets a test run before it is scripted.**

## Inputs the skill asks for

The skill asks in one message, and only for what's missing:

1. Which demo: what gets typed, and what should happen?  
2. Which tool, and which model or setting?  
3. What is the one message?  
4. Which background colour: orange, light green or dark green?

Then it writes the script and **shows it to Saskia for approval before recording.**

## The six beats

| Beat | Time | Job |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Hook** | 0–4s | A claim that challenges an assumption, in 10 words at most. Shown as on-screen text and spoken. |
| **Demo** | 4–18s | Type the prompt and show the output. |
| **Twist** | 18–22s | The moment it fails or surprises. |
| **Why** | 22–32s | The mechanism, in one or two short sentences. At most one analogy. |
| **Takeaway** | 32–37s | What this means for you, as an action the viewer can do tonight. |
| **Invite** | 37–40s | One spoken line pointing to the her/ai workshops and webinars. The end card shows workshop.herai.work. Never give dates. |

## Voice-over rules

- **Neutral narrator.** The narrator has no persona: never "I" or "we". Speak to the viewer ("Ask it…", "you"), or state what happens ("New chat."). Only the invite line names her/ai.  
- **Tone: sober and honest, with a dry wink.** Show the limit, then say what to do with it. No hype, no doom, and at most one joke per reel.  
- **80–90 words in total**, including the invite. At ElevenLabs' natural pace that is about 35 seconds of speech, which leaves the screen room to breathe.  
- **Sentences of 12 words at most**, in plain words. Explain any term in the same breath: "a token, one piece of a word".  
- **The voice never just repeats the screen.** It adds the punchline or the why. Reading the typed prompt aloud is fine, because on a phone it is too small to read.  
- **Be precise.** Say "nearly always" when results vary. Don't say the model *thinks, knows, understands* or *remembers*. Say it *predicts, was trained on, uses a tool*.  
- **Never use:** game-changer, revolutionary, unlock, supercharge, magic, mind-blowing, secret, hack.  
- **Write for the ear:**  
  - no symbols in the voice-over ("ten plus ten", not "10 \+ 10")  
  - check how ElevenLabs says "her/ai", and add a pronunciation rule if it gets it wrong

## Analogy bank (from the her/ai workshops)

Use one analogy per reel at most, and only if it makes the Why beat faster.

| Analogy | Explains |
| :---- | :---- |
| **Bread baked once.** The model comes out of the oven and doesn't change until the next one is baked. | The training cut-off; why it doesn't learn from your chats |
| **A dice roll for every word** | Why the same question gets different answers |
| **A toddler guessing "you're 200"**: right size of number, wrong number | Why a language model doesn't calculate |
| **A battery draining to red** | The context window filling up in a long chat |
| **"Don't stomp in the puddles"** makes a toddler stomp | Say what you *do* want, not what you don't |
| **A mirror**: AI reflects its data, and the data reflects us | Bias |
| **An intern on day one** | How much context to give |

## On-screen elements

- **Background:** the chosen brand colour, with the screen recording centred on it in vertical 9:16 format.  
- **Hook text:** shown from 0 to 4 seconds in the title font (Myeongjo, per the design system).  
- **Subtitles:** the full voice-over, burned in, in the body font (DM Sans).  
- **Green wiggle:** exactly one per reel, under the **highlight phrase** when it appears in the subtitles.  
- **End card:** from 37 to 40 seconds, showing "Workshops & webinars · workshop.herai.work".

## Recording rules (for computer use)

- Start on the tool's interface: logged in, in a fresh chat, with the sidebar hidden. No names, emails or chat history should be visible.  
- Logging in never happens on camera. If a login is needed, pause, ask Saskia to log in, and cut that part.  
- Change settings off-camera, unless flipping the setting is part of the demo.  
- Type prompts at a readable pace. Leave the key output on screen for 1.5–2 seconds.  
- **Cutting waiting time is fine. Changing the output is not.** Never edit, fake or stage what the AI produced.  
- If a take doesn't show the point, re-run it up to 3 times. If it still doesn't, rewrite the voice-over to match what actually happened, or drop the demo.  
- **For bias demos, use the first result.** Re-rolling until the result looks worse is staging.  
- The voice-over must match the take. If the script says "Four men" and the grid shows three, change the line.

## What the skill outputs for every script

1. Title  
2. Message  
3. Tool  
4. Background colour  
5. Word count  
6. Hook text  
7. Highlight phrase  
8. Pre-state: how things are set up before recording  
9. A beat table with timing, screen actions for computer use, and voice-over  
10. A fallback for when the take doesn't cooperate  
11. The Instagram caption

## Instagram caption format

1. **Line 1:** the hook.  
2. **2–3 short lines:** what happened and why. The caption can go one step deeper than the voice-over, including the shadow side.  
3. **"Try it yourself:"** 2–4 numbered steps.  
4. **Invite:** "This is one of the things we unpack in our her/ai workshops and webinars. Sign up via the link in bio."  
5. **5–8 hashtags:** always \#herai \#AIliteracy, plus the tool and the topic.

## 1\. It doesn't remember you

**Message:** The model has no memory. Memory is a feature built around it. **Tool:** ChatGPT · **Background:** dark green · **Voice-over:** 83 words **Hook text:** ChatGPT doesn't remember you. · **Highlight:** "starts from zero" **Pre-state:**

- Settings → Personalisation: switch off **both** "Reference saved memories" and "Reference chat history".  
- Open a fresh chat.

| Beat | Time | Screen | Voice-over |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Hook | 0–4s | Empty chat | ChatGPT doesn't remember you. Watch. |
| Demo | 4–18s | Type: *Today for lunch I had bread with avocado and an egg.* The reply appears. Click **New chat**. Type: *What did I have for lunch today?* | Tell it what you had for lunch. … Now open a new chat. Ask what you ate. |
| Twist | 18–22s | The reply says it doesn't know. Hold. | No idea. Every chat starts from zero. |
| Why | 22–32s | Settings → Personalisation, showing the memory toggles off. Toggle on, then off again. | Memory is switched off here. This is the model as it really is. Memory is a feature built around it. It saves notes about you for new chats. |
| Takeaway | 32–37s | Back to the chat | Handy. But those notes sit on a server. Check your settings tonight: decide what it keeps. |
| Invite | 37–40s | End card | More of this in her/ai workshops and webinars. Link in bio. |

**If the take doesn't cooperate:**

- *It remembers the lunch:* one of the two toggles is still on. Fix it and record again.  
- *It guesses a lunch:* keep the take. Change the Twist line to "It guesses. Every chat starts from zero."

**Caption:**

ChatGPT doesn't remember you. 🥑

Tell it your lunch, open a new chat, ask again: nothing. The language model itself has no memory; every chat starts from zero. "Memory" is a separate feature that saves notes about you and adds them to new chats. Handy, but those notes are stored on a server.

Try it yourself:

1\. Settings → Personalisation → switch off memory and chat history

2\. Tell it what you had for lunch

3\. Open a new chat and ask what you ate

4\. Switch memory back on and check what it has saved about you

## 2\. It doesn't calculate, it guesses

**Message:** A language model predicts words. The calculator is a tool built around it. **Tool:** ChatGPT, using a model that visibly shows its thinking and code (confirm this in the test run) · **Background:** orange · **Voice-over:** 81 words **Hook text:** AI doesn't calculate. It guesses. · **Highlight:** "most likely next word" **Pre-state:** Open a fresh chat.

| Beat | Time | Screen | Voice-over |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Hook | 0–4s | Empty chat | AI doesn't calculate. It guesses. |
| Demo | 4–18s | Type: *What is 10 \+ 10?* The instant reply is "20". Hold. Type: *What is 48,317 × 6,294 ÷ 17 − 883?* | Ten plus ten. Twenty, instantly. It didn't calculate. Twenty is simply the most likely next word. … Now a hard one. |
| Twist | 18–22s | The thinking indicator appears and a code block opens. Cut the wait. | See that? It's writing a small piece of code. A calculator. |
| Why | 22–32s | The answer appears. Scroll back to the code. | That calculator isn't the language model. It's a tool built around it. Alone, the model is a toddler guessing your age. Right size, wrong number. |
| Takeaway | 32–37s | Hold on the code | For numbers that matter, say: calculate this with code. |
| Invite | 37–40s | End card | Model and tools, explained: her/ai workshops and webinars. Link in bio. |

**If the take doesn't cooperate:**

- *It answers the hard sum without code:* check the answer with a calculator. If it's wrong, keep the take and change the Twist line to "Confident. And wrong." If it's right, re-run with a thinking model.  
- *It uses code even for 10 \+ 10:* say so honestly ("It even builds a calculator for this one") and keep the Why beat.

**Caption:**

AI doesn't calculate. It guesses. 🧮

Ten plus ten: instant, because "20" is simply the most likely next word. A hard sum: it stops, writes code and runs it. That calculator isn't the language model; it's a tool built around it.

Try it yourself:

1\. Ask "What is 10 \+ 10?"

2\. Ask a big sum, and watch whether it writes code

3\. For taxes, invoices, anything that matters: say "calculate this with code"

## 3\. Ask for a CEO, get a man

**Message:** Bias is a pattern in the training data, not an opinion. **Tool:** Midjourney · **Background:** light green · **Voice-over:** 82 words **Hook text:** Ask AI for a CEO. · **Highlight:** "most likely" **Pre-state:** Logged in, on the create screen. **Use the first grid; don't re-roll.**

| Beat | Time | Screen | Voice-over |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Hook | 0–4s | Prompt bar | Ask AI for a CEO. Guess who shows up. |
| Demo | 4–18s | Type: *A photo of a CEO in a meeting room*. Submit, and cut the generation wait. | A CEO in a meeting room. Nothing else. |
| Twist | 18–22s | The four images appear. | Four images. Four men. *(match this to the take)* |
| Why | 22–32s | Slow pan across the grid | Nobody told it to pick a man. It learned from millions of pictures. In those, CEOs are mostly men. So it gives you the most likely answer. That's bias. |
| Takeaway | 32–37s | Type: *A photo of a female CEO in a meeting room*. The grid appears. | Want something else? Say so. And notice what it assumes when you don't. |
| Invite | 37–40s | End card | Spotting bias is a skill. her/ai workshops and webinars, link in bio. |

**If the take doesn't cooperate:** the result is "nearly always" men. If the grid shows a woman, keep it and narrate it honestly: "Mostly men. One woman, out of four." Never re-roll.

**Caption:**

Ask AI for a CEO. Guess who shows up. 👔

Nobody told it to pick a man. It learned from millions of pictures, and in those, CEOs are mostly men. So it gives you the most likely answer. That's bias: not an opinion, a pattern in the data. And the data reflects us.

Try it yourself:

1\. Open any image generator

2\. Ask for "a CEO", "a nurse", "a pilot", "a scientist"

3\. Notice who it shows you, and who it doesn't

## 4\. It only knows what it has seen

**Message:** Less training data means a worse result, and the data is not spread evenly across the world. **Tool:** Midjourney · **Background:** orange · **Voice-over:** 80 words **Hook text:** AI is only as good as what it has seen. · **Highlight:** "it guesses" **Pre-state:**

- Logged in, on the create screen.  
- **Test first that Midjourney allows "President Ruto"**, because it has blocked some heads of state.  
- For the Dutch version, use King Willem-Alexander.

| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |

Type: *A photo of President Ruto in a yellow raincoat*. | Queen Elizabeth in a yellow raincoat. Spot on. Nearly always the older Queen. That's what most photos show. … Now President Ruto of Kenya. |
| Twist | 18–22s | The images appear; zoom on a face. | That's not him. |

AI is only as good as what it has seen. 🌍

Try it yourself:

## 5\. Same question, three answers

**Message:** AI works on probability, not a database. Every word is a dice roll. **Tool:** ChatGPT · **Background:** dark green · **Voice-over:** 81 words **Hook text:** Same question. Three different answers. · **Highlight:** "dice roll" **Pre-state:** Switch web search off, so it predicts rather than looks things up. Open a fresh chat.

| Beat | Time | Screen | Voice-over |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Hook | 0–4s | Empty chat | Ask AI the same question twice. Get two answers. |
| Demo | 4–18s | Type: *What's the best time to post on Instagram? One sentence.* Answer 1 appears. Click **Regenerate**; answer 2 appears. Regenerate again; answer 3 appears. | Best time to post on Instagram. A clear answer. … Ask again. … And again. |
| Twist | 18–22s | Show the three answers side by side (1/3, 2/3, 3/3). | Three confident answers. Three different times. |
| Why | 22–32s | Hold on the three answers | It's not looking anything up. It predicts one word at a time. Every word is a dice roll between likely options. So the answer shifts each time. |
| Takeaway | 32–37s | Hold | When it matters, ask twice. If the answers differ, check a source. |
| Invite | 37–40s | End card | More of this in her/ai workshops and webinars. Link in bio. |

**If the take doesn't cooperate:** if the three answers are near-identical, use a more open question, for example *What's the healthiest breakfast? One sentence.*

**Caption:**

Same question. Three different answers. 🎲

AI doesn't look up a stored answer. It predicts one word at a time, and every word is a dice roll between likely options. That's why the person next to you gets a different answer, and why "it said so" isn't proof.

Try it yourself:

1\. Ask any question with search off

2\. Hit regenerate twice

3\. Compare the three answers

## 6\. Confidently wrong

**Message:** AI writes what sounds right, not what is true. **Tool:** ChatGPT or Claude, with web search off · **Background:** light green · **Voice-over:** 80 words **Hook text:** Ask AI about a book that doesn't exist. · **Highlight:** "what sounds right" **Pre-state:**

- Switch web search off.  
- **Before recording, search to confirm that the title and author really don't exist.**  
- Open a fresh chat.

| Beat | Time | Screen | Voice-over |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Hook | 0–4s | Empty chat | Ask AI about a book that doesn't exist. |
| Demo | 4–18s | Type: *Summarise the main argument of "The Quiet Algorithm" by Marieke Hoeksma (2019).* | A title, an author, a year. All made up. |
| Twist | 18–22s | A confident summary appears. Hold. | And it writes you a summary. No doubt, no warning. |
| Why | 22–32s | Scroll the summary | It doesn't check whether things are true. It writes what sounds right. A title plus an author sounds like a real book. So it fills in the rest. |
| Takeaway | 32–37s | Hold | Facts, names, sources: always check them. Or ask it to search. |
| Invite | 37–40s | End card | her/ai workshops and webinars. Link in bio. |

**If the take doesn't cooperate:** newer models often answer "I'm not familiar with that book." In that case:

1. Try *Give me three academic studies on women and AI adoption, with links*, then open a link on screen. Dead or wrong links prove the same point. Change the Demo and Twist lines to match.  
2. If it behaves well both times, drop this demo.

**Caption:**

Ask AI about a book that doesn't exist. 📚

It may write you a confident summary anyway. A language model doesn't check facts; it writes what sounds right. A title plus an author sounds like a real book, so it fills in the rest. This is called hallucinating.

Try it yourself:

1\. Switch web search off

2\. Invent a book title and author

3\. Ask for a summary, and see what it does

## 7\. Stuck in the past

**Message:** A model's knowledge stops at its training cut-off. Anything newer only comes in through a search tool. **Tool:** Claude or ChatGPT (Claude's web search toggle is the easiest to control) · **Background:** orange · **Voice-over:** 80 words **Hook text:** AI doesn't know what happened last week. · **Highlight:** "baked once" **Pre-state:**

- Switch web search off.  
- Pick a neutral event from the last 1–2 weeks: a sports final, a launch or an award. Nothing political or tragic.  
- Open a fresh chat.

| Beat | Time | Screen | Voice-over |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Hook | 0–4s | Empty chat | AI doesn't know what happened last week. |
| Demo | 4–18s | Type: *When were you last trained?* A date appears. Type: *Who won \[recent event\]?* | Ask when it was last trained. There's a date. … Now ask about last week. |
| Twist | 18–22s | It doesn't know, or answers with outdated information. | It doesn't know. Or worse, it guesses. |
| Why | 22–32s | Switch web search on and ask again. The answer appears with sources. | A model is baked once, like bread. Then it's used for months. Anything newer comes in through a tool: web search. Now it looks it up. |
| Takeaway | 32–37s | Hold on the sources | For anything recent, tell it to search. And check the sources. |
| Invite | 37–40s | End card | More of this in her/ai workshops and webinars. Link in bio. |

**If the take doesn't cooperate:**

- *It searches on its own:* change the Twist line to "It went searching. That's a tool, not its own knowledge."  
- *It already knows the event:* pick a newer one.

**Caption:**

AI doesn't know what happened last week. 📅

A model is trained once, then used for months. Its knowledge stops at that date. Anything newer only comes in through a tool like web search, and without one it may simply guess. (Also a good reason not to ask a chatbot who to vote for.)

Try it yourself:

1\. Ask "When were you last trained?"

2\. Ask about last week, with web search off

3\. Switch search on, ask again, and check the sources

## 10\. The doctor is a he

**Message:** Bias lives in text as well as images. **Tool:** Google Translate, with ChatGPT as the fallback · **Background:** orange · **Voice-over:** 80 words **Hook text:** Swahili has no "he" or "she". · **Highlight:** "most common" **Pre-state:** Open Google Translate set to Swahili → English, with an empty box.

| Beat | Time | Screen | Voice-over |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Hook | 0–4s | Empty translator | Swahili has no he or she. Watch what AI does. |
| Demo | 4–18s | Type: *Yeye ni daktari. Yeye ni muuguzi.* The translation appears. | Yeye means he or she. Swahili doesn't choose. … The doctor. The nurse. |
| Twist | 18–22s | Zoom on the English output. | The doctor became a he. The nurse, a she. |
